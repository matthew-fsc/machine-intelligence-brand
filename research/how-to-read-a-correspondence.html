How to read a correspondence

Every claim that the brain and the model line up gets one of three words, and a paragraph that says where it stops being true.

reading time · 4 min depth · primer last updated · 2026-08-13

Every claim on this site that the brain and the machine line up gets one of three words. The word is a promise about how far you can push the comparison before it lets you down. Learn the three words and you can read any piece here at a glance.

the fidelity scale
RatingWhat it claimsHow far to trust it
mechanismThe same causal process, not just the same shape.Load-bearing. You can reason from one side to the other.
analogyA structural match that runs through different machinery.Useful. It predicts and transfers intuition, with care.
metaphorA figure of speech, alike on the surface only.For intuition only. Do not use it to explain results.

Mechanism is rare and reserved. It means the two systems are running the same process, confirmed, not just producing similar behavior. When a piece rates something a mechanism, you are allowed to carry a fact from the brain to the machine and expect it to hold.

Analogy is where most of the useful comparisons live. The structure matches and the intuition transfers, but the underlying machinery differs, so you keep one hand on the railing. An analogy is a good source of predictions and a bad source of proofs.

Metaphor is a resemblance on the surface with unrelated causes underneath. That is not a dismissal. A metaphor can be the best tool for generating hypotheses, as long as you never let it explain why something happened.

Where the mapping breaks

Every essay carries a section with exactly that title, and it is not an afterthought. It is the test the rest of the piece has to pass. Stating where a comparison fails is how you find out whether it was a mechanism, an analogy, or a metaphor all along. A claim that cannot survive its own limits does not earn a place here.

what to do with a rating

Read the rating first, then the limits section, then decide how much of the comparison to carry into your own work. The rating tells you the ceiling; the limits section tells you the edges.

A worked habit

When you meet any brain-to-machine claim, here or elsewhere, ask the fidelity question before you accept it. Is someone asserting the same mechanism, a useful analogy, or a vivid metaphor? Most overclaiming in this field is a metaphor wearing a mechanism's coat. The three words are a quick way to check the coat.
