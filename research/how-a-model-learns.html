How a language model learns, in plain terms

A language model is trained to do one narrow thing, guess the next word, billions of times, until the guessing encodes a great deal about the world.

reading time · 5 min depth · primer last updated · 2026-08-13

A language model can feel like it is doing many things: answering, reasoning, writing, planning. Underneath, it was trained to do one narrow thing, over and over, until the doing of it soaked up a surprising amount of the world. That one thing is guessing the next word.

the key idea

The model is trained to predict the next token given everything before it. Do that across enough text and the prediction machinery has to encode grammar, facts, and a good deal of structure, because that is what makes the next word predictable.

One job: predict the next token

Feed the model a stretch of text and it produces a probability for every possible next token, a token being roughly a word or a piece of one. During training it makes that guess, sees the real next token, and gets a score for how close it was. Nudge the model to raise the probability of the right answer, repeat billions of times, and the guessing gets very good.

What weights are

The model's knowledge lives in its weights: a vast set of numbers that determine how the input is transformed into that next-token guess. Training is the slow adjustment of those numbers. When this site's thesis names weights as the machine half of the seam, this is what it points to. The weights are fixed once training ends; the model does not rewrite them while it is talking to you.

Why predicting words teaches so much

It sounds too simple to work. The trick is that predicting the next word well requires understanding a great deal of what the words are about. To finish "the capital of France is" you need a fact. To finish a proof you need its logic. To keep a story consistent you need to track who did what. The prediction objective quietly forces the model to represent all of that, because there is no other way to keep guessing correctly.

What it is not

Two cautions that the deeper essays develop. The model has no separate reasoning module that switches on for hard problems; the same next-token process produces the careful answer and the careless one. And low error on word prediction is not proof of a correct inner model of the world, only of good surface prediction. Keeping those two facts in view is most of what separates careful use of these systems from wishful use.
